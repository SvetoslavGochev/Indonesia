1. Komodo National Park
Komodo is one of Indonesia's most iconic parks. It combines dry island landscapes, blue sea, coral reefs, and the famous Komodo dragon.
Why visit:
- The only natural habitat of wild Komodo dragons.
- Excellent diving, snorkeling, and island viewpoints.
- Dramatic and highly photogenic scenery.

2. Bromo Tengger Semeru National Park
This park is the volcanic heart of Java, with mountain ridges, sand seas, active craters, and legendary sunrise views.
Why visit:
- Iconic sunrise over Mount Bromo.
- Fresh mountain air and striking volcanic landscapes.
- Great options for hiking and photography.

3. Ujung Kulon National Park
Ujung Kulon is one of Indonesia's most important protected areas. Dense forests, quiet coasts, and wetlands shelter rare wildlife.
Why visit:
- Home of the critically endangered Javan rhino.
- Wild forests and peaceful coastal views.
- High conservation value and unique biodiversity.

4. Gunung Leuser National Park
One of Sumatra's best known tropical regions, rich in rainforest, rivers, mountains, and primates including orangutans.
Why visit:
- Chance to observe orangutans in the wild.
- Vast tropical biodiversity.
- Ideal for adventure travel and ecotourism.

5. Lorentz National Park
Lorentz is Southeast Asia's largest national park, spanning alpine zones, wetlands, tropical forests, and coastal ecosystems.
Why visit:
- Exceptional ecosystem diversity in one park.
- One of the world's most impressive protected landscapes.
- Strong scientific and conservation importance.

6. Tanjung Puting National Park
Tanjung Puting is known for river routes, mangrove areas, and orangutan habitats. Boat travel here is a classic jungle experience.
Why visit:
- River cruises through tropical rainforest.
- Opportunity to see orangutans and other wildlife.
- A memorable eco-adventure in Borneo.
