Bat Bayan - The Protective Brother and Last Pillar of Old Great Bulgaria

In an age of migrations, imperial clashes, and dramatic fractures in the early medieval world, the name Bat Bayan remains in Bulgarian memory as a symbol of duty, sacrifice, and brotherly responsibility. As the eldest son of Khan Kubrat, founder of Old Great Bulgaria, he carried the heavy burden of protecting his people in one of the darkest moments of their history.

Legacy of a Great Father

When Khan Kubrat died around the middle of the 7th century, he left his five sons a powerful but increasingly threatened state. His will urged the brothers to remain united and rule in agreement so they could preserve their strength. As the eldest, Bat Bayan took the central role, inheriting the core lands around the Kuban River and the Sea of Azov.

But the geopolitical storm came quickly. Pressure from the Khazar Khaganate in the east became an overwhelming threat.

Separation and a Difficult Choice

Under Khazar pressure, the brothers chose different paths to preserve the Bulgarian people from total destruction. This was the moment Old Great Bulgaria was divided:

Kuber moved south toward Pannonia and later Macedonia.
Asparuh led part of the tribes westward and founded Danubian Bulgaria.
Kotrag moved north toward the middle Volga, where Volga Bulgaria emerged.

Bat Bayan, however, chose to stay.

As the eldest and lawful ruler of the central core, he accepted the difficult role of guardian of the old lands and of those who could not, or did not want to, leave. Remaining in the homeland meant enduring subordination and severe hardship.

The Protective Brother in the Storm

In the North Caucasus, Bat Bayan fought an unequal struggle for survival. He was forced to enter vassal relations with the Khazars to protect his people from massacre and complete erasure. Although later historical sources associate him with political compromise, his image in collective memory remains clear: the protective brother who gave up personal glory and freedom so that part of the Bulgarian population could survive.

His mission was quiet but heroic - to hold what remained in the ancestral lands and keep alive the spark of identity while his father's state collapsed under the pressure of history.

Historical Significance

Bat Bayan is the opposite of a conqueror. He represents the archetype of the defender who remains at his post while others march toward new horizons. Without his sacrifice and willingness to bear the burden of defeat and compromise, an entire generation of Bulgarians around the Sea of Azov might have vanished without a trace.

Bat Bayan remains in history not only as Kubrat's firstborn son, but as the last pillar of Old Great Bulgaria - a true protective brother whose memory deserves deep respect.
