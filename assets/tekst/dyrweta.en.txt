1. Ulin (Eusideroxylon zwageri)
Habitat: Lowland tropical forests in Kalimantan (Borneo) and parts of Sumatra.
Key traits:
- Known as "ironwood" because of its extremely dense and durable timber.
- Slow-growing tree that can live for centuries.
- Used for bridges, piers, and structures exposed to moisture and insects.
Conservation: Vulnerable due to illegal logging and slow natural regeneration.

2. Amber Tree (Dipterocarpus spp.)
Habitat: Tropical forests of Sumatra, Borneo, Java, and Sulawesi.
Key traits:
- Large forest tree that can reach around 70 meters.
- Source of dammar resin used in varnish and adhesives.
- Important for wildlife because it provides food and shelter.
Conservation: Threatened by logging.

3. Teak (Tectona grandis)
Habitat: Java, Sumatra, and Maluku.
Key traits:
- Fast-growing tree that can reach around 40 meters.
- Valued for high-quality timber in furniture and construction.
- Naturally resistant to rot and insects.
Conservation: Pressured by overharvesting.

4. Mangrove Trees (Rhizophora spp.)
Habitat: Coastal zones of Sumatra, Java, Borneo, and Papua.
Key traits:
- Grow in brackish coastal water with aerial roots.
- Protect shorelines from erosion and storms.
- Support fish nurseries and bird habitats.
Conservation: Threatened by land conversion and aquaculture.

5. Casuarina (Casuarina equisetifolia)
Habitat: Coastal areas of Java, Sumatra, and Maluku.
Key traits:
- Fast-growing tree with needle-like branchlets.
- Used for timber, fuelwood, and erosion control planting.
- Tolerates drought and salty soils.
Conservation: Relatively stable, but dependent on coastal habitats.

6. Banyan (Ficus benghalensis)
Habitat: Tropical forests of Java, Sumatra, and Borneo.
Key traits:
- Giant tree with aerial roots that form additional trunks.
- Strong symbolic role in Indonesian culture.
- Provides shade and food for birds and primates.
Conservation: Affected by habitat fragmentation.
