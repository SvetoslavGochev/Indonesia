The Battle of Doiran (1917): Triumph of the Bulgarian Spirit
The Battle of Doiran in 1917 was a key episode of World War I and the second major victory of the Doiran defensive epic. Fought from April 22 to May 9, 1917, it became a symbol of the military skill of the Ninth Pleven Infantry Division under General Vladimir Vazov.

Strategic context
After recognizing that Bulgarian positions were strongly fortified, British command relied on overwhelming artillery power to break the line. Three divisions were concentrated, supported by heavy guns and mortars. On the Bulgarian side, the Ninth Pleven Division prepared for a determined defense.

Course of the battle
The clash began with a massive four-day artillery bombardment. Despite serious damage to forward positions, Bulgarian units answered with continuous fire.

Infantry attacks: During the night of April 24 to 25, British infantry attacked and temporarily captured key points, but Bulgarian counterattacks pushed them back with heavy losses.

Valley of death: In the following days, repeated assaults were repelled. A new large offensive on May 8 ended in complete failure. Because of severe casualties, British soldiers referred to one of the sectors near Point Boris as the valley of death.

Result and historical significance
On May 9, unable to achieve a breakthrough, British command ended the attacks. The battle ended with a decisive Bulgarian victory.

The success at Doiran in 1917 preserved the front and strengthened the reputation of the Bulgarian soldier and General Vladimir Vazov as among the most capable commanders of their time.
