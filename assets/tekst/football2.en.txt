Indonesian Club Football: Leagues, Teams, and Participation in Asian Competitions
1. Main leagues in Indonesia
The Indonesian league system has several levels, with the most important being:

League	Tier	Teams	Season	Notes
Liga 1	1st	18 teams	February-December	Top professional division
Liga 2	2nd	24 teams	February-December	Semi-professional
Liga 3	3rd	Regional clubs	Varies	Amateur level

Best-known teams in Liga 1 (2024):

Persija Jakarta (capital club, one of the most popular teams)
Arema FC (famous for the "Aremania" fan base)
Persib Bandung (one of the most successful clubs)
PSM Makassar (2023 champions)
Bali United (well-funded and consistently competitive)
Madura United (representing Madura Island)
Borneo FC (club from Kalimantan)
2. Participation in AFC competitions
Indonesian clubs mainly compete in two AFC tournaments:

Tournament	Format	Qualification	Indonesian representatives (2023/24)
AFC Champions League	Top-tier Asian club competition	Champions of stronger leagues plus qualifiers	PSM Makassar (Liga 1 champions 2022/23)
AFC Cup	Second-tier continental competition	Clubs from developing leagues or cup paths	Persija Jakarta, Arema FC (qualified in 2023/24)

How qualification works:
AFC Champions League (ACL):

The Liga 1 champion gets the main slot.
If licensing criteria are not met, the slot can pass to the next eligible club.
In 2023/24, PSM Makassar played in qualification rounds but was eliminated.
AFC Cup:

Teams qualify via preliminary rounds or domestic cup positions.
Persija Jakarta and Arema FC played in the 2023/24 AFC Cup group stage.
Historical achievements in Asia
Best AFC Champions League-era results:

1995 - Persib Bandung reached the quarterfinals (then under a different competition format).
2007 - Arema FC reached the quarterfinals in continental play.
Best AFC Cup-era result:

2010 - Arema FC reached the semifinals.
3. Challenges for Indonesian club football
Advantages:

Large and passionate fan base, especially in Jakarta, Bandung, and Makassar.
Growing interest in youth development and emerging talent.
Increasing private investment in selected clubs.
Problems:

Governance and integrity issues have affected competition stability in some periods.
Infrastructure gaps remain, with many venues still below top international standards.
Limited long-term planning, with some clubs relying too much on short-term signings.
Strong competition from European and other Asian leagues for audience and talent.
4. Future outlook
Development plans through 2026 are focused on league quality, infrastructure, and youth systems.
The national football ecosystem is receiving more attention from both institutions and fans.
More modern stadium projects and operational upgrades are expected in coming seasons.
Interesting fact:
Persija Jakarta's "Jakmania" is among the largest supporter groups in the region and frequently travels with the team.
Arema FC is known for one of the loudest matchday atmospheres in Asia, with drums, chants, and visual displays.
