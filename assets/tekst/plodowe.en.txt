Here are the top 6 traditional Indonesian fruits you should try! They are delicious and widely used in local cuisine, drinks, and desserts.

1. Mangosteen - "The Queen of Fruits"
📍 Where to find it: Grows in tropical regions of Indonesia, especially Sumatra, Java, and Kalimantan.
🍽 How to eat it:
The fruit has a dark purple rind that opens easily.
Inside is white, juicy flesh with a sweet and slightly tangy taste.
Usually eaten fresh, but also used in salads and desserts.
💡 Interesting:
In Indonesia it is considered one of the tastiest fruits and is called "the queen of fruits".
Mangosteen juice is also used in traditional drinks like es campur.

2. Jackfruit - The giant of tropical fruits
📍 Where to find it: Found across Indonesia, especially in Sumatra and Java.
🍽 How to eat it:
The fruit can grow very large (up to 30 kg).
Ripe jackfruit is sweet and eaten fresh.
Unripe jackfruit is used in dishes like gudeg from Java.
The seeds can be boiled or roasted.
💡 Interesting:
In Indonesia jackfruit is called nangka and appears in many traditional dishes.
It is also used in drinks such as es teler.

3. Rambutan - "The Hairy Fruit"
📍 Where to find it: Found throughout Indonesia, especially Sumatra, Java, and Sulawesi.
🍽 How to eat it:
It has a red or yellow peel with soft "hairs".
The inside is white, juicy, and sweet.
Peel it open and remove the seed before eating.
💡 Interesting:
The name rambutan comes from the Indonesian word rambut, meaning "hair".
It is commonly eaten fresh and also used in desserts.

4. Longan - A small tropical favorite
📍 Where to find it: Grows in Indonesia, especially Sumatra and Java.
🍽 How to eat it:
The fruit is small and round with a thin brown shell.
The flesh is white, juicy, and sweet.
The shell is easy to remove and the seed is discarded.
💡 Interesting:
Longan is often used in traditional drinks like es longan.
In some Asian cultures it is linked with luck.

5. Salak - "Snake Fruit"
📍 Where to find it: Grows in Indonesia, especially Java and Sumatra.
🍽 How to eat it:
It has a hard, scaly skin that looks like snake skin.
The inside has segments, each with a large seed.
Its taste is sweet and tangy, similar to apple and pineapple.
💡 Interesting:
The name salak is related to the fruit's snake-like texture.
It is mostly eaten fresh and also used in fruit mixes.

6. Durian - "The King of Fruits"
📍 Where to find it: Found in Indonesia, especially Sumatra, Java, and Kalimantan.
🍽 How to eat it:
The fruit is large and covered with spikes.
Inside is creamy flesh with a very strong smell.
The taste is rich and sweet, though the aroma is intense.
💡 Interesting:
Durian is famous as the "king of fruits", but its smell is so strong that it is often restricted in hotels and public transport.
In Indonesia it is considered a delicacy and used in many desserts.

Conclusion:
These six fruits are only a small part of Indonesia's rich fruit diversity. Each has a unique flavor and place in local cuisine.
