Khan Tervel | The Savior of Europe at the Gates of Constantinople
In the early Middle Ages, Europe faced an existential threat that could have changed its entire future. At that critical moment, the young Bulgarian state and its ruler stood against one of the strongest military forces of the age.

The shadow from the East
At the start of the eighth century, the Arab Caliphate was expanding rapidly across the Middle East, North Africa, and the Iberian Peninsula. Its strategic goal was to continue into Europe. In 717, a massive Arab army and fleet moved against Constantinople, hoping to turn it into a base for further advance into Central and Eastern Europe.

The siege was long and brutal. The Byzantine Empire was pushed to the edge, and it seemed that nothing could stop the invasion by land and sea.

The intervention of the Bulgarian lion
At this decisive moment, Khan Tervel entered the conflict. Recognizing the scale of the danger, he acted in support of his ally and in defense of the continent.

During the winter of 717 and the spring of 718, the Bulgarian army struck the rear of the besieging Arab forces. In key clashes, Bulgarian troops inflicted heavy losses, disrupted supply lines, and broke the offensive momentum of the Caliphate. Winter conditions, hunger, and repeated attacks weakened the invaders.

By the summer of 718, the siege was lifted, and the surviving Arab forces retreated.

Historical significance
Tervel's victory near Constantinople had major consequences for Europe. It blocked a large-scale Arab expansion toward Eastern and Central Europe and gave the continent valuable time for political and cultural development.

Although the battle directly involved Byzantium, its geopolitical impact reached far beyond one empire. Khan Tervel remains in world history not only as a successful ruler, but as a shield who defended European civilization in one of its darkest hours.
