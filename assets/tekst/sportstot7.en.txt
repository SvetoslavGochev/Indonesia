The Most Popular Sports in Indonesia

Indonesia has a rich sports culture where traditional and modern sports exist side by side. Below you can find the most widely practiced sports in the country.

1. Football (Sepak Bola) – national passion

Why is it so popular?
Football is a national passion in Indonesia. It is much more than a sport – it is part of everyday life, conversations, and local identity. From large cities to smaller islands, people watch matches, support their clubs, and create a strong fan culture. Matches of the national team (Garuda) and local clubs attract huge interest. Liga 1 Indonesia is the top football division in the country.

Main clubs:
• Persija Jakarta – one of the most popular
• Persib Bandung
• Persebaya Surabaya
• Arema FC

Derbies between major teams attract huge interest and make the championship very emotional.

Interesting facts:
• Indonesia is the only Southeast Asian country that has played at a FIFA World Cup (1938).
• The stadiums are known for a colorful atmosphere, choreography, and strong fan support.

Main stadiums:
• Gelora Bung Karno (Jakarta) – national stadium
• Gelora Bandung Lautan Api (Bandung)

Atmosphere at the stadiums:
Fans often sing throughout the match, and home games become a major event for the entire city. Attending a match is an experience that is hard to forget.

How to watch a match in Indonesia:
• Visit coffee shops (warung kopi) or sports centers
• Check the official club channels for schedule and tickets
• Arrive early due to traffic around the stadium
• Choose seats in family sectors if it's your first time
• Cafeterias offer local snacks and drinks

How to play:
In many cities, you can play on public pitches for free or for a small fee. This is an excellent way to connect with the local community.

The national team:
Indonesia's national team (Garuda) has a young generation of players and growing ambition for development. Interest in every international match is huge, and the sports community closely follows performance in regional tournaments.

International tournaments: AFC Champions League (ACL) and AFC Cup

Indonesian clubs participate in two main Asian tournaments organized by the Asian Football Confederation (AFC):

AFC Champions League (ACL) – Asia's Championship
ACL is the most prestigious club tournament in Asia, similar to the European Champions League. It features champions from strong Asian leagues.

Best Indonesian performances in ACL:
• Persija Jakarta – participated in ACL group stages
• Persib Bandung – regular group stage participant
• Arema FC – quality tournament appearances

Although Indonesian clubs participate, they usually do not advance past the group stage due to strong competition from Japanese, South Korean, and Chinese teams.

AFC Cup – Asia's second tournament
AFC Cup is the second most important club tournament in Asia and is more accessible for Indonesian clubs.

Best Indonesian performances in AFC Cup:
• Persija Jakarta – reaching semifinals and group stages
• Persib Bandung – regular participant
• Sriwijaya FC – significant tournament appearances
• Other clubs also participate regularly

Indonesia has better chances in AFC Cup, where some clubs have managed to advance past the group stage and progress further.

Why is international competition important?
• Helps clubs develop and play against strong opponents
• Gives local players the opportunity to showcase their talent at the Asian level
• Increases experience and popularity of local football

Conclusion:
Football in Indonesia is energy, community, and emotion. Attending a match there is a real experience. International competition through ACL and AFC Cup shows the growing ambition of Indonesian football on the Asian continent.

2. Badminton (Bulu Tangkis) - Indonesia's national sport
Why is it so important?
Badminton is a national sport and one of Indonesia's most successful disciplines. Indonesian players such as Taufik Hidayat and Susi Susanti are world champions.

Interesting fact:
Indonesia has won Olympic gold medals in badminton at almost every Olympic Games since 1992 (except 2012).

Where can you play?
Clubs: PB Djarum, PB Tangkas (Jakarta).
Public courts: Many parks and sports centers offer courts for rent.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to learn badminton, look for youth badminton clubs that offer beginner lessons.

3. Volleyball (Bola Voli) - a sport for all ages
Why is it popular?
Volleyball is accessible and easy to practice. You will see it on beaches, in schools, and on community courts.

Interesting fact:
Indonesia also has mountain volleyball, played at higher altitudes where the air is thinner.

Where can you play?
Beaches: Kuta Beach (Bali), Pantai Losari (Makassar).
Public courts: Many city parks have volleyball courts.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to play volleyball in Indonesia, look for friendly local matches (pertandingan persahabatan).

4. Pencak Silat - traditional martial art
Why is it important?
Pencak Silat is a traditional Indonesian martial art practiced for centuries. It includes strikes, grappling, and the use of weapons such as the kris.

Interesting fact:
Pencak Silat is included in the Asian Games and is one of the world's oldest martial arts.

Where can you train?
Schools: IPSI (Ikatan Pencak Silat Indonesia) and many local sports centers.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to understand Pencak Silat better, watch documentaries and visit cultural festivals in Indonesia.

5. Cycling (Balap Sepeda) - growing popularity
Why is it popular?
Cycling is becoming more and more popular, especially among younger people. Indonesia offers many mountain and flat routes suitable for riding.

Interesting fact:
Indonesia hosts Tour de Indonesia, an international cycling event.

Where can you ride?
Mountain routes: Mount Bromo (East Java), Mount Rinjani (Lombok).
City bike lanes: Jakarta, Bandung, and Surabaya have dedicated bike lanes.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to start cycling, look for local cycling clubs or bike shops that organize rides and lessons.

6. Tennis (Tenis) - popular among the middle class
Why is it popular?
Tennis is widely available in private and public clubs, especially in large cities.

Interesting fact:
Indonesia has hosted major international tennis events and has produced notable players.

Where can you play?
Clubs: Jakarta Tennis Club, Bandung Tennis Club.
Public courts: Many city parks offer paid tennis courts.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to learn tennis, search for tennis academies or beginner classes.

7. Rowing (Dayung) - a sport with tradition
Why is it important?
Rowing is a traditional sport in Indonesia, especially in regions such as Kalimantan and Sulawesi where boats are part of daily life.

Interesting fact:
Indonesia hosts rowing festivals and regattas such as Pesta Laut.

Where can you practice?
Rivers and lakes: Lake Toba (Sumatra), Rawa Pening (Central Java).
Clubs: Jakarta Rowing Club.

Teacher Tip:
If you want to try rowing, look for rowing clubs or canoe and kayak lessons.
