"Borneo Travel Story: Jungles, Orangutans, and the Deep Soul of Kalimantan"

🌿 Why Borneo?
Borneo, known in Indonesia as Kalimantan, is the third-largest island in the world and one of the last truly wild places on the planet. Here, among untamed jungles, crystal rivers, and communities that still live with a strong connection to tradition, you can experience nature in its purest form.

This travel story will take you from the lively city of Pontianak to the heart of Betung Kerihun National Park, where orangutans roam free and overnight stays in longhouses can take you back in time.

🗓️ A 3-day route: from civilization to jungle
(A balance of culture, adventure, and rest)

Day 1: Pontianak - The gateway to Borneo
📍 Arrival in Pontianak

The airport: Pontianak's Supadio Airport is small but convenient. From there, you can take a taxi into the city ($3-5) or use Grab/Gojek.
Stay: Choose a guesthouse in the center ($10-15/night) or a hotel with a view of the Kapuas River ($30-50).
📍 What to do in Pontianak?

Pontianak market: One of the largest markets in West Kalimantan, where you can buy local spices, woodcarvings, and traditional clothes.
Kapuas River: The longest river in Indonesia! Rent a longboat ($5-10) and cruise along the water.
Xuan Kong Temple: The oldest Chinese temple in Borneo, built in 1776.
🍽️ Food in Pontianak

Try soto banjar — a traditional chicken soup with coconut milk.
Pizza at Pizza e Birra — a surprisingly good pizza spot in the jungle!
Kue lapis — a layered coconut cake sold on every corner.
💡 Tip: Pontianak is not a major tourist destination, so do not hesitate to negotiate prices.

Day 2: Expedition to Betung Kerihun - Meeting the orangutans
✈️ Flight to Putussibau

Airlines: Take a domestic flight with Wings Air or Susi Air ($50-80 one way). The flight takes about 1 hour and offers amazing views over the jungles.
Arrival in Putussibau: A small town where you can hire a jeep or motorcycle taxi for the trip to the national park ($10-15).
🚣‍♂️ Trip to the national park

Kapuas River boat: A two-hour longboat ride ($10-15) through green valleys and villages where people live in longhouses.
On foot to the camp: From the dock to the Betung Kerihun camp, it is a 2-3 hour walk through the jungle. Bring rain gear, mosquito repellent, and a full bottle of water.
🏕️ Overnight in the jungle

Betung Kerihun camp: Usually a tent or wooden hut overnight stay ($20-30). Dinner is included, usually rice with fish or chicken, plus breakfast.
Night walk: If you are brave, you can join local guides for a night walk to see nocturnal animals such as lemurs and snakes.
🌿 What to expect in Betung Kerihun?

Orangutans: The largest population on the island! They live high in the trees, but sometimes come down to the ground.
Proboscis monkeys: These small primates with long noses are extremely curious.
Giant squirrels: The largest squirrels in the world, some reaching the size of a dog!
💡 Tip: Bring binoculars and a good camera with low-light performance — the jungle is dark!

Day 3: Longhouse and return to civilization
🏡 Visit a longhouse

What is a longhouse? It is a traditional Dayak communal home where an entire village lives together. Longhouses can be up to 150 meters long and house hundreds of people!
What to do there?
Talk to the village elders — they will tell you about traditions, medicinal plants, and the historic headhunting wars (yes, this was a real part of life until the 20th century!).
Try local tuak — a palm-sap fermented drink with a mild sour taste.
Buy handmade souvenirs such as wooden masks or woven baskets.
🚤 Return to Pontianak

Kapuas River boat: Return by longboat ($10-15), this time with the sunset over the jungle.
Last night in Pontianak: Visit Kedai Kopi Tionghoa for Chinese-Indonesian food and strong coffee.
✈️ Flight to your next destination

If you are heading to Bali or Java, take a night flight from Pontianak ($60-100).

💡 How to save money in Borneo?
Food:
Eat at local warungs instead of hotel restaurants.
Try street food like soto banjar or kue lapis.
Accommodation:
Stay in guesthouses or homestays ($10-15/night).
Skip expensive Pontianak hotels — they are not worth it.
Transport:
Use local ride-hailing apps (Grab/Gojek) instead of private drivers.
Agree on the price before getting into the boat or taxi.
Activities:
Plan excursions in advance for discounts.
Bring your own gear (for example a rain jacket) instead of renting it.
🌍 Useful Borneo tips
📌 Health and safety
Vaccinations: Recommended for hepatitis A and B, typhoid, and meningitis. Check with your doctor.
First aid kit: Bring antimalarial tablets if you are traveling to malaria-risk areas, plus bandages, antiseptic, and diarrhea medicine.
Water: Drink only bottled water - river water is not safe.
Mosquitoes: Use DEET repellent and wear long sleeves after sunset.
📌 Culture and etiquette
Clothing: In Pontianak you can wear what you want, but in longhouses and villages cover your shoulders and knees.
Photography: Ask permission before taking photos of people or ceremonies. Some communities do not like being photographed.
Gifts: If you visit a longhouse, you can bring food (for example rice or canned goods) or small gifts such as soap or clothes.
📌 Technology and connectivity
Internet: Pontianak has 4G, but there is no signal in the jungle. Buy a local SIM card (Telkomsel or XL Axiata) for $5.
Electricity: Most guesthouses and hotels have 220V, but there may be no power in the jungle.
Camera: Bring a waterproof case for your camera - you will want to photograph waterfalls and river life!
